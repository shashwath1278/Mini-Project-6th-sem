
Paper 3 Analysis: ML for Plastic-Degrading Enzyme Prediction

Overview:
The research focus is using deep learning to address plastic pollution by identifying microbial enzymes from bacteria and fungi that can degrade synthetic polymers.
The goal is to leverage protein language models and CNNs to screen amino acid sequences for "degrading potential" much faster than traditional wet-lab testing.


Current Limitations:

1.Accuracy vs. Diversity: Prediction accuracy tends to drop significantly as the pairwise sequence identity and diversity of protein sequences increase.

2.Resource Intensity: State-of-the-art architectures, such as TResNet or EfficientNet, require very high GPU resources for both training and inference.  

3.Performance Gap: There is a notable gap between genomic database predictions and how enzymes actually perform in messy, real-world environments 
  like soil or anaerobic conditions


Proposed Enhancements:

1.Architecture: Combine ProtBert-encoded features with Bi-LSTM layers. This helps the model capture local biochemical structures as well as long-range
  sequence context for more reliable classification.

2.Fine-Tuning: Implement Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) to specialize the model for specific plastic types, such as PET or PLA, 
  without the need for full retraining.

3.Metadata Integration: Incorporate global microbiome metadata to refine screening filters. Identifying where these microbes naturally thrive helps
  predict which enzymes will remain functional under specific environmental conditions.
